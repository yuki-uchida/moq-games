# アニマフロント (ANIMA FRONT) — design

An original 3D third-person action shooter. Plush-toy-cute animals who inherited a ruined human world fight an
alien invasion with **Antiques (アンティーク)**: cute, animal-shaped weapons powered by **Anima (アニマ)**, a
mysterious energy left behind by humans. Two ways to play:

- **防衛任務 (PvE)**: a squad of 4 against hordes of invaders (alien creatures, alien machines, foreign animals).
- **三つ巴演習 (PvP)**: 3 squads × 4 in a simulated city.

Every empty seat is filled by a bot, so both modes are fully playable alone. Up to 4 humans (PvE) or 12 humans
(PvP) can play together over a MoQ relay.

This file is the contract every module is written against. Code comments are English; all player-facing text is
Japanese. Names, characters, story, art and sounds are original: never use terms or designs from existing works
(e.g. no "trigger", "trion", "bail out", "neighbor", "BETA", "hive", "-class" enemy ranks, "Chameleon", "Spider",
"Grasshopper", "Bagworm", "Scorpion", "Egret", "Ibis").

## 0. Pillars

1. **Anima is life and ammunition.** One pool: damage removes it, every shot, swing, gadget and armour spends it.
2. **16 slots, total freedom, real consequences.** Any antiques in any of the 16 slots. Expensive builds are
   allowed and run dry mid-fight; the editor shows the predicted sustain so players learn to mix in cheap ones.
3. **Two paws, two antiques.** The right paw (左クリック) and the left paw (右クリック) each hold one active antique;
   `Q` / `E` cycle them. Shield in one paw and pistol in the other, two pistols, sword and grapple…
4. **Cute animals, grim war.** Round plush animals with candy-coloured weapons in a world that is losing: military
   briefings, ruined cities swallowed by violet crystal, a last fortress city.
5. **Mobility.** High jumps, dashes, frog pads, gecko hooks and gliders: fights happen across streets and rooftops.

## 1. Platform and layers

- Directory `examples/games/anima-front/`. Vite config `vite.config.anima-front.mjs` (dev port **5198**), also an
  input of the site build. TypeScript + three.js 0.180, no other runtime dependency, no framework.
- **No model, image, font or audio files.** Models are generated by code (SDF sculpting → meshes), textures are
  drawn into canvases, music and sound are synthesized with Web Audio. Fonts: the system Japanese gothic stack.
- Storage keys start with `anima-front-v1:`. Relay app id `anima-front-v1`.

| layer  | folder        | role                                                                                          |
| ------ | ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| data   | `src/data/`   | static tables: species, antiques, enemies, missions, presets, story text. Pure, shared by all |
| sim    | `src/sim/`    | deterministic rules at 60 Hz, maps' collision data, enemies, bots. No DOM, no three.js        |
| models | `src/models/` | procedural model kit (SDF → BufferGeometry), characters with skeletons, procedural animation  |
| render | `src/render/` | three.js scene, map visuals, actors, effects, camera, post-processing, quality                |
| audio  | `src/audio/`  | `GameAudio` (`api.ts`): music, one-shot effects, loops. Synthesized                           |
| input  | `src/input/`  | keyboard + mouse (pointer lock), gamepad, touch → `ControlState`                              |
| ui     | `src/ui/`     | DOM screens and HUD (CSS in `src/ui/*.css`)                                                   |
| net    | `src/net/`    | sessions (solo / host / guest), wire format, validation, interpolation, prediction            |
| app    | `src/app/`    | boot, screen flow, settings, saves, wiring of every layer                                     |

Rules between layers:

- `sim` imports only `data` and its own files. It never knows about particles, cameras, sounds or text. It reports
  what happened as `SimEvent`s inside the `View` (`src/sim/types.ts`), and the client decides how that looks/sounds.
- `render`, `ui` and `audio` read the `View` (and the static `MapDef`), never the live `World`.
- View-side randomness never touches the sim RNG. The sim is deterministic for a given seed and input sequence.
- `models` imports only `three` and `data`. `render` imports `models`.

## 2. Units and conventions

- 1 world unit = 1 metre. **Y is up.** Ground level is y = 0; rooftops and platforms are boxes.
- Fixed step `DT = 1/60` s. Snapshots 20 Hz, guest inputs 30 Hz.
- **Yaw** 0 faces **+Z**; forward = `(sin yaw, 0, cos yaw)`; right = `(−cos yaw, 0, sin yaw)`. Turning right
  _decreases_ yaw (mouse right → `yaw -= dx·sens`). **Pitch** > 0 looks up, clamped to ±1.35 rad.
  Aim direction = `(sin yaw·cos pitch, sin pitch, cos yaw·cos pitch)`.
- Models are built facing +Z with their feet at the origin; the renderer sets `rotation.y = yaw`.
- Positions in views are tuples `[x, y, z]` of the character's **feet**.
- Team ids: PvP 0, 1, 2. PvE: every player is team 0; enemies are `'foe'`.

## 5. Anima rules (`src/sim/anima.ts`)

- `anima` ∈ [0, maxAnima], starts full. Max = species anima (+ enhancements).
- **Damage** (after armour) removes anima. **Costs**: an active antique checks `anima ≥ cost + 1` before it acts
  (the last point is never spent by your own actions; a failed use plays the "空" click and flashes the gauge).
- **Upkeep**: passive antiques (armour, some gadgets, enhancements with upkeep) drain per second while online.
- **Critical (≤ 15%)**: every passive antique goes **offline** (no effect, no upkeep) and the HUD says
  「アーマー維持限界」. They come back online above 20%.
  This includes the **enhancement options** (タカノメ, チーター, コアラ, ナマケモノ, キバ, サイノカワ, ラクダ): their damage,
  spread, cost, rate, regeneration, speed, dash-cooldown and max-anima bonuses all stop too, so costs and stats fall
  back to the bare animal until anima is above 20% again (the sim reads them through `modsOf`; ラクダ's max anima
  shrinks while offline but never below 1 current anima).
- **Regeneration**: after 4.0 s with no spending and no damage taken, +1.5/s (enhancements add). PvP team base:
  +12/s at any time, no delay. PvE: defeated enemies drop anima shards (`ANIMA_DROP` in enemy data) that fly to
  the nearest player within 6 m; hamsters get ×1.5.
- **アーマー崩壊** at 0:
  - PvE: the animal is **down** (生身) for up to 15 s: moves at 35% speed, cannot use antiques, beacon visible to
    allies. An ally holding interact (F) within 2.5 m for 2.0 s **revives** it: the reviver pays 15 anima, the
    downed animal returns with 30% anima and 1 s of invulnerability. If nobody does, it is **away** (帰巣): it
    warps out, waits 8 s and respawns at the squad's base with full anima, using one of the squad's **転送枠**
    (respawn tickets: 訓練 8, 正規 4, 決死 3). No tickets left → it stays away. All players down/away with no
    tickets → mission failed.
  - PvP: the armour breaks and the animal warps out at once (KO). The team that dealt the last damage within 10 s
    scores; respawn after 5 s at the team base with full anima and 2 s of protection.
- No self-damage and no friendly fire.

## 7. Loadout (`src/sim/loadout.ts`, `src/data/presets.ts`)

- A loadout is exactly **16 slots** (a 4×4 board). Each slot holds one antique or is empty. Active antiques also
  name a paw: `R` (right, left click) or `L` (left, right click).
- Rules: at least one weapon (melee or ranged) in some paw; an active antique at most once per paw (the same
  antique in both paws is allowed: e.g. two ミツバチ fire alternately); an enhancement at most 3 times; any other
  passive at most once. Invalid loadouts are repaired by `sanitizeLoadout` (drops the offending slots).
- Each paw's active antiques form that paw's list in slot order (slot 0 → 15). `Q` / `E` (or the wheel for R)
  cycle a paw; switching takes 0.25 s (the antique dissolves and re-forms in light).
- **Sustain estimate** (editor): `fight = upkeep + Σ per paw (cost rate of its first weapon × 0.5 duty) + 9/s
incoming × (1 − armour reduction)` minus regeneration; shows **推定継戦時間** = anima / fight (∞ if ≤ 0) and a
  bar of anima use by category. Also shown: max anima, upkeep/s, cheapest/most expensive actions.
- Six presets (one default per species, all selectable): **アタッカー** (シャチマル + ミツバチ…), **ガンナー**
  (キツツキ, ダンゴムシ…), **スナイパー** (フクロウアイ, ヤモリフック, モモンガ…), **タンク** (アルマジロ, カメノコウラ,
  マンモス…), **サポート** (ミツツボアリ, コウモリ, ヤドカリ, ハチドリ…), **遊撃** (ネコノツメ, イッカク, カエルパッド…).
  Players keep 3 custom loadouts per species (saved).

## 9. Movement and physics (`src/sim/physics.ts`, `src/sim/player.ts`)

- Static world = axis-aligned boxes (`MapDef.boxes`) on a uniform grid for queries. Characters are vertical
  capsules; they slide along walls and step up ledges ≤ 0.45 m. Map edges are walls.
- Ground speed 6.8 m/s × species × modifiers; acceleration 60 m/s² on the ground, 16 m/s² in the air; gravity
  26 m/s²; jump velocity 11.5 × √jump → ≈ 2.5 m high (rabbit ≈ 3.4 m); coyote time and jump buffer 0.1 s.
- Dash: 0.2 s at 21 m/s in the move direction (or forward), cooldown 0.9 s, cost 0.5, one air dash per jump.
- Frog pads launch to 13 m; gecko hook pulls at 26 m/s; gliders and owls fall at 2.2 m/s. No fall damage.
- Aim pivot = feet + (0, eye height, 0) + right × shoulder offset × side. The aim ray starts at the pivot. The
  camera sits behind the pivot on the same line, so the crosshair is exactly the aim ray. Projectiles start at
  the paw's muzzle (pivot-relative offsets in `src/sim/constants.ts`) and fly toward the aim ray's first hit
  (or 250 m along it).

## 10. Combat (`src/sim/combat.ts`, `src/sim/projectiles.ts`)

- Projectiles are simulated (swept spheres) against actor hit volumes and boxes. The beam is a per-tick ray.
- Melee hits use a sector test in front of the attacker (yaw only), reach and arc from the antique.
- Damage pipeline: base × enhancements × species trait → target modifiers (guard/shield, jelly negate, armour %,
  front armour of brutes, weak points ×2) → apply. Every application produces a `hit` event (amount, head, kind).
- Knockback: blasts 6 m/s, hammer knock-up, brute charge 9 m/s; bears take 40%.
- Kill credit: the last damager within 10 s; assists: other damagers within 10 s.

## 11. Enemies (`src/data/enemies.ts`, `src/sim/enemies/`)

Behaviours run on the host. Masses use a flow field toward their target (the objective or the nearest player)
plus separation; special units steer individually. Every attack is telegraphed (wind-up state ≥ 0.35 s).

### 三つ巴演習 (PvP, `src/sim/modes/pvp.ts`)

- Map `arena` (ユメクジラ演習場): a virtual city with three bases 120° apart around a central tower plaza.
- 6:00. KO = +1 for the scoring team; the last 60 s (**ラストスパート**) KOs are +2. Respawn 5 s, 2 s protection.
- Team bases: regeneration +12/s; foes inside a base take 20/s (no spawn camping).
- Highest score wins; a tie plays 60 s of sudden death (first KO wins), then a draw.
- Teams: **アカネ隊** (crimson `#e5484d`), **アオイ隊** (blue `#3e8ed0`), **キハダ隊** (yellow `#f2b33d`), plus
  pattern stripes on armour (diagonal / dots / chevrons) so colour is never the only cue.

Map `range`: static and moving targets, a dummy squad, a tutorial track (move → look → jump → dash → paws →

## 13. Bots (`src/sim/bots/`)

Bots use the same input surface as humans (`PlayerInput`). Skill 0..1 sets reaction time (0.6 → 0.15 s), aim
error (6° → 1°) and tactics. They navigate a ground grid (A\*) plus map jump pads, pick targets by threat and
distance, choose the antique by range and remaining anima (cheap weapons below 40%), dash away from wind-ups,
revive allies, return to regenerate or collect shards when low, and use gadgets heuristically (honey when allies
are hurt, wall when under heavy fire, sonar on contact, pads to reach rooftops). In PvE they stay within ~25 m
of the squad; in PvP they move between lanes and contest the plaza.

## 15. Rendering and art direction (`src/render/`, `src/models/`)

- **Characters**: soft rounded SDF-sculpted single meshes with skeletons, vertex-colour markings, baked ambient
  occlusion, a 3-band toon ramp with rim light, ink outlines. Expressive faces: glossy eyes with two highlights,
  brows, small mouth, blush. Procedural animation: idle breathing, run with squash, jump/fall, dash lean, aim
  (upper body follows pitch/yaw), recoil, melee swings per antique, guard, hurt flinch, down (sitting dazed in a
  bare state), revive, warp-out, victory poses, emotes in results.
- **Antiques**: chunky, cute, readable silhouettes of their animal; glowing accent lines; a muzzle anchor.
- **Enemies**: creatures are glossy and organic with pulsing veins; machines are clean ceramic with light seams.
  Large crowds are drawn with instancing.
- **World**: a ruined human city reclaimed by plants (cracked asphalt, moss, ivy, trees through roofs, rusty
  cars, faded signs with invented text) plus animal-made additions (paper lanterns, rope bridges, paw-print
  banners). The blight is crusted with violet crystal; the arena is clean and glowing.
- Lighting per map: day (warm sun), sunset, night (moon, lanterns, anima glow), blight (violet haze), virtual
  (cool and bright). Hemisphere + sun with soft shadows, fog, gradient sky with clouds.
- Post-processing: tone mapping, bloom on emissive anima, anti-aliasing, vignette; quality presets 低/中/高 and
  自動 (drops when frames exceed 22 ms for 3 s).
- Camera: shoulder TPS (arm 3.2 m × species scale, pivot on the aim line), spring-arm collision, smoothing, recoil
  kick, FOV kick on dash, zoom for the owl sniper with an owl-eye scope overlay, light shake on blasts (off with
  reduced motion), hit-stop 40–60 ms on melee hits.
- Every `SimEvent` has a visual answer: muzzle flashes and tracers in the antique's colour, impacts per surface
  (concrete dust, metal sparks, violet ichor splats, shield ripples), toon explosions, damage numbers (setting),
  hit markers, anima shards flying to the collector, armour shatter, warp beam, antique materialize/dissolve,
  dash streaks, pad springs, hook lines, glider membranes, camouflage shimmer, sonar ring, ink clouds, honey glow.

## 16. Sound (`src/audio/`)

- `GameAudio` (`src/audio/api.ts`), synthesized (no files), never throws. Every `SimEvent`, menu action and
  staging beat has a sound; positional sounds use pan + distance volume from the camera.
- Music: title, menu, briefing, oldtown (defence), harbor (machines), night raid, blight, boss, final boss (two

## 18. Networking (`src/net/`)

- `CooperativeNetwork` from `examples/games/rabbiters/src/network.ts`, app `anima-front-v1`, host-authoritative.
- Lobby messages carry seats, animals, loadouts and teams; the host validates every loadout with
  `sanitizeLoadout` and every input field by field.
- Host: steps the world at 60 Hz (a worker clock keeps it running in a hidden tab), sends snapshots at 20 Hz
  (quantized arrays, events since the last snapshot). Guests: interpolate others 100 ms behind, predict their own
  movement (`createPredictor`) and reconcile, send input at 30 Hz with edge counters (`presses`) so no jump or
  swap is lost or doubled.
- Up to 4 humans in PvE and 12 in PvP. A member arriving after the start drops into a free seat (a bot's, or one
  whose player left) with its own animal, loadout and name (`World.replace`: it warps in next to the squad in PvE,
  at its team's base with spawn protection in PvP, keeping the seat's scoreboard); PvP prefers its chosen team,
  else the team with the fewest players. Only when every seat is held by a player does it spectate. A human
  leaving is replaced by a bot. A PvP spectator picks the team it watches in the game screen (team buttons, keys
  1–3, LB / RB; 「切り替え中…」 until that team's key arrives) and steps through that team's animals. When a seat
  frees up during the match (the host's lobby state counts them), a spectator takes it from the spectator bar
  (「空いた席で参加」: ProfileMsg.j asks for this match; the host seats it like a drop-in). The host
  removes spectators like seated members, in the lobby and from the in-match menu's 参加者 panel. Spectating
  records nothing (no mission clear, best rank or PvP result); the results say it was 観戦.
- **PvP information boundary.** The relay is anonymous and the host's track is one broadcast anyone can read, so
  a PvP snapshot carries only a public part in clear (clock, scores, map props, events that are neither about a
  player nor positional, the kill feed, a name/bot/KO roster) plus one part per team sealed with that team's
  AES-GCM key (`src/net/split.ts`, `seal.ts`). A team part holds its members, the enemies it may know about, their
  events, shots and shards, and its input acks. Player-placed props, resting shards and positional effects are
  scoped per team too: its own team's props always, an enemy prop or a resting shard when within 6 m or in line of
  sight of a living member (the same blockers) or — a prop — while its owner is known (decoys with the 0.4 s
  hysteresis, walls, pads, honey, ink and shards known until gone; the owner only when known); impacts and blasts
  of known players, others' only within 45 m (+ the blast radius) of a living member and without the actor; prop
  damage on player props only to the teams that know the prop. The host recomputes the sets for every snapshot
  (`src/net/visibility.ts`): an enemy is known within 6 m of a living member, in line of sight (boxes, ヤドカリ
  walls, ink; eye, aim pivot or camera to head or chest; ≤ 150 m; camouflage ≥ 0.5 only within 5 m; 0.4 s
  hysteresis), when that team's sonar reveals it, when it fired or used an antique in the last 1.5 s within 45 m,
  when it stands in that team's base or was hit by that team in the last second; everyone in the results. Team
  keys reach guests wrapped with an ECDH P-256 + HKDF key (guest key in its profile, host key in the lobby state)
  and are replaced every match and whenever a holder leaves, is removed or a seated player changes team. A
  spectator may switch the team it follows (at most once a second) inside a generation and then gets that team's
  key: spectators are room members the host admitted and can remove, like observers in a custom match. Anyone who
  knows a guest's id can read that guest's track too, so guest → host messages are sealed with a guest-to-host
  key from the same ECDH secret (another HKDF purpose): PvP inputs, and the reconnection secret in every mode
  (bound to the member id, so a replay from another id gets no seat). A guest's ECDH key is fixed once the host
  knows it. Guests draw unknown enemies as off the field and fade enemies out of / into sight (`fog.ts`); the
  host's own screen keeps to the same boundary. Enemy name tags and HP bars need a clear line from the camera past
  the map's boxes and the placed ヤドカリ walls (`src/render/markers.ts`) unless sonar revealed them. PvE snapshots
  and inputs stay whole and unsealed.

## 19. Performance budget

- Desktop target 60 fps at 中: ≤ 450 draw calls, ≤ 700k visible triangles, sim step ≤ 2.5 ms with 120 foes.
- Limits: 12 players, 120 foes, 300 projectiles, 2000 particles. Models are generated once per kind (cached).
