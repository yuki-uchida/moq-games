# デスガイヤ (Ember Grid)

Independent, local, single-player tactical example: lantern wardens cross six frontiers to disperse the ash pack. No network or SDK initialization is required. Open `/moq-games/examples/games/ember-grid/` after `npm run dev`. The root Vite build includes this separate HTML entry; the gallery is managed separately.

## Story presentation

The prologue, chapter title cards, introductions and victory scenes use the characters' runtime names and rendered 3D portraits. Lines belonging to fallen allies are omitted; the classic ending remembers fallen allies even after continuing a save, while casual has no memorial. Tap or press Enter/Space to complete and advance a line; the skip button or Escape closes the scene. Turning story off skips dialogue but retains chapter title cards and credits. Reduced motion disables type-on and title animation. Credits identify the repository's owner-provided models and music and generated sound effects; they do not imply additional asset rights.

The six-chapter campaign includes Luka's chapter-four introduction, the final dragon encounter, the epilogue and credits.

Victory updates the turn and objective before the outro; the report follows the dialogue.

Story checks: `npx vitest run examples/games/ember-grid/tests/*.test.ts --maxWorkers=1` and `E2E_PORT=5271 npx playwright test --config examples/games/ember-grid/playwright.config.ts`. Story screenshots are saved under `test-results/story-*.png`.

## Expedition

- **峡谷の燈**: a long rock spine has a one-square central pass and longer flanks around its northern and southern ends. Near-side groves shelter the approach; a wolf and scout screen the pass, with an armored watchman behind them.
- **風抜けの水路**: staggered stone shelves split the field into winding northern and southern lanes. Central groves link the approaches; two wolves, a scout and an armored watchman threaten the far bank.
- **灰の環城**: broken walls enclose a grove-studded courtyard with a western gate and a northern breach. Wolves threaten the northern edge while a ranged scout guards the southern approach.
- **霧の湿原**: seize the shrine at (8,4), or rout the enemy. 癒し手ルカ joins here.
- **骸の礼拝堂**: survive through the enemy phase of turn 6, or rout the enemy.
- **灰の玉座**: defeat the ash dragon, or rout the enemy.

The title screen offers a new expedition, continue, reached chapters, settings and help. Choose **カジュアル** to restore fallen allies after victory with their growth up to their fall, or **クラシック** for permanent loss. Difficulty stays fixed for the expedition and is shown in the status and mission list. Unreached chapters are locked. Victory opens a chapter-clear report and offers the next mission; the sixth victory completes the expedition. Chapter 3 also ends when its boss falls. Seizing requires a living ally on the shrine when the ally phase ends; all chapters also end in victory on a rout, and losing all allies always loses. Standard formation moves three squares; guard formation gains four maximum HP per ally but moves only two squares. The guard bonus is not carried.

ルカ uses a staff instead of attacking: select her, then an injured living ally at Manhattan distance 1–2 to restore up to 8 HP, end her action, and gain 10 EXP. She joins when starting chapter 4; her survival and growth are carried from chapter 4 onward. Replaying chapter 4 from its start checkpoint brings her back, while a loss in a won chapter removes her from subsequent checkpoints. Starting a new expedition clears all checkpoints.

The stage loads the deployed roles for the current chapter and keeps decoded sources in memory; the following chapter's models are prefetched after preparation. Chapters 1–3 use soldier, archer, mage, goblin and wolf; chapter 4 uses the surviving allies plus healer, slime and bat; chapter 5 adds wraith, shaman and beetle; chapter 6 uses ogre, dragon, wraith and wolf. The five original roles total 49,266,969 bytes of distinct model and motion sources; healer/slime/bat add 15,481,220 bytes, wraith/shaman/beetle 15,821,464 bytes, and ogre/dragon 16,421,836 bytes. These are raw source sizes, not compressed transfer sizes; a direct chapter selection loads only its actual roster and may omit previously listed roles. Sound effects are prepared separately for the active roster. Staff healing uses the healer's authored motion, while ranged attacks launch from model emitters where available. A patterned tile denotes seizure, and the status bar shows the current objective. Music changes with the chapter.

Enemies use three deterministic modes: charge moves toward allies, guard stays until damaged or an ally enters movement plus attack range and then charges permanently, and hold never moves. They prefer a lethal hit, then the highest damage, then the lowest remaining HP, preferring groves when position scores tie. No reinforcements are used.

Select an ally, then a glowing tile to move; selecting an outlined enemy opens a forecast. Confirm with 攻撃 or select the same enemy again; やめる or Escape cancels without an action. The card shows portraits, HP before and after, damage, lethal hits and relevant modifiers. Red squares show where the selected ally could strike after any legal move (or from its current square once it has moved). Hovering, focusing or selecting an enemy without an ally selected shows that enemy's movement (muted blue) and strike range (red). The 敵の攻撃範囲 toggle adds a hatched union of all living enemies' next-phase attack ranges, recomputed after actions.

The forecast overlays the console rather than resizing the field, leaving both the attacker and target visible. On narrow screens the selection summary stays on one line and HP tags shift vertically if their screen-space rectangles overlap; the full unit details remain available through ⓘ.

Each unit moves once and attacks once per turn. Attacking, waiting or healing ends its action. ↶ 戻す undoes the selected unit's most recent move until it acts, another ally is selected, or the turn ends. A unit that has moved, has no enemy in range and cannot usefully heal (full HP or no potions) also counts as finished. Once every living ally is finished, the enemy turn begins automatically after the last action's presentation, except while the selected unit can still undo: ✓ 待機 is highlighted so one tap ends its action. End Turn remains available to pass earlier. Range uses Manhattan distance (including through rocks); there are no counterattacks. Groves reduce incoming damage by two, to a minimum of one. Units may walk through squares held by their own side but must stop on an empty square; rocks and opposing units block movement. Enemies follow the same rule, crossing each other but not allies. Walk animations follow the same path.

Bows deal +3 against wolves, swords +1 against goblins. Shield-marked enemies reduce physical damage by two; magic bypasses their armor. Attack previews include terrain, armor and matchup effects. The two shared recovery potions each restore up to eight HP and consume that unit's action. Healing too early wastes supplies; healing instead of attacking can expose the rest of the team.

Defeat all enemies or meet the chapter objective to win; lose all allies to lose. Survival counts completed enemy phases. Replay clears the current encounter, including a pending enemy sequence. A phase banner labels each new phase (after the chapter's intro scenes at a chapter start). During the enemy phase, ⏩ or tapping the field speeds remaining presentations to roughly three times normal and removes gaps between enemies; the tactical results are unchanged. HP tags include a bar, number and side shape. Right-click or long-press a unit, or use ⓘ for the selected unit, to inspect its stats and matchup notes; Escape or 閉じる dismisses the detail and restores focus. The board is one Tab stop: arrow keys move between cells, Enter/Space chooses, and Escape cancels the forecast or selection. Help is available through ?. Mission selection also provides brief tactical hints. Human completion time has not been measured.

## View

The camera (`src/camera.ts`) opens each battle as close as it can while keeping every unit in view (up to about 64 px tiles, never below the whole-board fit), and selecting an ally eases in on its moves (after it moves, on its reach) without zooming out. ＋, － and the whole-board button in the field corner, the mouse wheel (zooming at the pointer), a two-finger pinch and the + / − / 0 keys zoom; dragging with a mouse or one finger pans, and a drag never acts on a tile. Enemy moves and strikes off screen bring the camera along, each ally phase brings the allies back into view, and keyboard focus pans to the focused tile. The view is applied as CSS variables on the field (`--zoom` enlarges the floor and tag grids, `--pan-x/y` shift them) and the 3D camera re-reads the same DOM geometry after every change, so floor, figures and tags stay aligned; figures keep their whole-board-fit size boost so they scale exactly with the floor. The field clips instead of scrolling, so focus or `scrollIntoView` can never move the floor away from the 3D layer. Reduced motion jumps instead of easing.

On touch devices, the ⊞ button enables optional selection assistance: tap the approximate tile, then choose the intended tile from the larger labelled buttons for it and its neighbors. The chooser occupies the console rather than the battlefield; scroll its choices if necessary or close it to cancel. It works at any zoom and preserves direct mouse and keyboard input. Long-pressing a unit still opens its details.

The 10×10 field is an oblique board in three layers. A square keeps the same projection on desktop and portrait while adding room for flanks; deployments remain close enough for early contact. The floor is the DOM grid of tile buttons, rotated −45° and squashed (`--tilt` 0.6, 0.95 on narrow portrait screens so tiles stay tappable); it carries terrain texture, range tints, selection/target outlines, focus and all pointer, touch and keyboard input. Above it, one transparent three.js canvas (`src/stage.ts`, `pointer-events: none`) draws everything that stands on the field — units, groves, rocks, contact shadows, side/selection rings, arrows and spells — depth-tested in one scene, so nearer trees, rocks and units overlap correctly at every step of a walk. Its orthographic camera reproduces the CSS floor projection exactly (45° azimuth, elevation `asin(--tilt)`, one world unit per unprojected tile, re-fitted on every resize). A second projected grid above the canvas (`#marks`) holds the HP/armour/✓/damage-preview tags, so they are never covered. Units face their walking step or their target; at rest allies and enemies face each other three-quarters toward the camera. Finished units are drawn dimmed.

The floor and surrounding terrain remain procedural CSS/SVG: a turbulence-noise ground texture, mission-specific earth, aqueduct stone or ash paving, one lantern light map sliced across tiles, bevelled tile seams, an earth slab with lit/shaded faces and faint drifting embers (disabled with reduced motion). The paving and surrounding terrain are decorative and do not affect movement. Groves (flat-shaded conifers and shrubs placed toward the back of the tile), faceted canyon and aqueduct rocks, and broken castle masonry are procedural low-poly three.js meshes with shared geometry and materials, lit by a moonlit sky, a cool key from the far-left and a warm lantern light on the ally flank. All rock and masonry tiles are impassable; no image asset is added.

The marsh adds reeds to blocked rocks, the chapel uses pillars and grave markers, and the throne room adds braziers; these props only occupy existing impassable cells and do not change movement. The seize tile has a pulsing star ring matched by the objective icon, while bosses have a separate gold ground ring and crown tag. Per-role board presentation scales keep small creatures smaller and bosses at 1.25 times their role scale; slime tint is muted without changing model files. Portrait rendering retains its original scale. On portrait title screens the three existing hero images sit above the menu; landscape keeps them beside it.

## Growth, carry-over and loss

Allies start at Lv 1 and gain EXP whenever their action lands: 10 per hit, plus a defeat bonus of 20, both scaled by the level gap (hit `max(1, 10 + 2·gap)`, defeat bonus `max(5, 20 + 5·gap)`, gap = enemy level − ally level) and 10 per recovery potion. Enemies are Lv 1–2 in the first mission, Lv 3–4 in the second and Lv 5–6 in the third, so later fights pay more. Every 100 EXP is a level up: HP (max and current), attack and defense each rise by one with a per-role chance (sword 80/45/45 %, bow 60/55/25 %, magic 50/65/20 %); if nothing rises, the role’s best-growth stat rises instead. Defense lowers all incoming damage (minimum one). Allies gain nothing while defending and enemies never gain EXP. Rolls use an injectable random source; append `?seed=N` to reproduce them.

After each action the acting ally’s EXP gauge fills; a level up follows with a window in which each raised stat lights with +1 in turn (synthesized Web Audio chime when sound is enabled, no new asset). The gauge alone never holds input; a level-up window briefly does. Neither intercepts pointer events.

Victory shows a chapter report: turns, defeats, survivors, remaining potions and, for each ally, level (▲ gained), EXP, EXP earned, HP/attack/defense and defeats. The MVP has the most defeats, then the most damage dealt; a surviving ally is always preferred over a fallen one.

Level, EXP and stats carry into the next mission; HP is fully restored and potions reset to two.

When an ally falls, a short farewell window shows their last words and “○○は倒れた…”. In classic mode, winning makes the loss permanent: the report marks them 離脱 and they do not deploy in the next mission. In casual mode, they return with their growth up to the fall. The start log and the ⚑ list show each mission’s deployed members (出撃) and lost ones (離脱). A single survivor can still start the next mission alone.

Each mission starts from a recorded party:

- Mission 1 always starts from the fresh Lv 1 party.
- Advancing from a victory records the grown party as the next mission’s start.
- Defeat, ↻ replay and ⚑ selection start the mission from its record, discarding EXP earned and undoing losses in the abandoned attempt (like an FE reset). Unreached missions cannot be selected. The selection list shows each start level and its members.
- If a lost ally is needed, ⚑ an earlier mission whose record still includes them; that also replaces the later records once you advance again.
- Completing the last mission and choosing “最初から遠征” confirms before clearing the expedition records, unlocked chapters and checkpoints. The versioned `ember-grid:save:v1` local save contains difficulty, formation, checkpoints, reached and cleared chapters, best turns, and an independent battle snapshot. A snapshot is written at each ally-phase start (including the initial deployment), not during actions or enemy turns; continuing rolls back to that safe point, including units, objective state and random state, and the title and help say so. Choosing a chapter from the title confirms before replacing a suspend after turn 1. Victory immediately saves the clear, best turn and next checkpoint and removes the suspend before dialogue; defeat clears the suspend after its presentation. The final victory records its date and party; the completed title offers ending replay, a new expedition or explicit chapter selection instead of silently restarting the finale. Older v1 saves remain readable; a v1 save that already lists the final chapter as cleared is recognized as completed, with its chapter-start party used for ending replay if no final snapshot exists. Unavailable storage or invalid saves show a notice and do not prevent play. Starting a new expedition confirms before replacing a save.

Settings are stored under `ember-grid:settings:v1`: BGM/SFX volume, battle speed, attack and heal confirmation, default danger zone, story scenes, tutorial and reduced motion. Changes apply live; battle speed composes with enemy fast-forward. The ♫ quick mute remains independent and persists under `ember-grid:sound`.

The board uses arrow keys to move focus and Enter or Space to act. Focus returns after presentations; Escape cancels a forecast or closes a dialog. Restarting or selecting a chapter asks before discarding a battle that has progressed; cancel preserves the battle and suspend save. An untouched starting battle needs no confirmation. The in-game reduced-motion setting disables decorative CSS motion independently of the operating-system preference.

The chapter-one tutorial follows the selected ally and available actions, including waiting or undoing a move with no target; a turn ending followed by the danger control completes it without requiring an attack. Restarting or revisiting chapter one starts unfinished guidance again. It highlights controls and legal targets without blocking play. Skip or complete it to remember the choice in `ember-grid:tutorial-complete:v1`; disabling it in settings hides it. The help dialog uses keyboard-navigable tabs. Heal forecasts show current and resulting HP, and share the attack confirmation and undo controls.

Later missions add one HP to every enemy, which carried growth offsets.

## Assets and sound

The title quietly reuses the first battle track, beginning only after the first gesture. Battle music stops for the report; synthesized victory, defeat and final-chapter motifs follow the SFX volume and quick mute. Screen changes fade without intercepting input; reduced motion skips the fade. If WebGL is lost, battle input is suspended with a notice until three.js restores its resources and the stage resynchronizes.

Units are the existing published rigged glTF models of the tower-defense game, loaded through its model catalog, `ModelAssetCache` and `ModelUnitVisual` actor (with their material/skinning adapters, foot separation and gait correction); no model, texture or animation byte is changed and no sprite sheet is used. The preparation overlay loads the deployed roles' motions and sounds. Geometry and textures are shared between actors; each actor owns its skeleton and material clones, and rigs are shader-compiled in the background. Failed requests leave battle input disabled and offer retry; successful sources stay cached. Idle units breathe at half frame rate (frozen under reduced motion); presentations render at full rate. The chapter report renders each ally's idle pose from its own model. No new public assets are added.

Movement and combat sample the models' own authored clips; logical combat rules remain synchronous and unchanged. Walks follow four-neighbour legal paths at 220 ms and one walk cycle per tile. Attacks play the attack clip at the tower game's recorded durations and contact times (`ATTACK_PROFILES`); arrows and spells leave the model's authored release point (bow string, staff tip) and fly for 240 ms before the target reacts. Hits flash and recoil the victim along the attack direction while a rising HP-loss number and the shared pixel contact spark appear as DOM overlays at the projected point; a defeat plays a rig-wide fall and fades out over 550 ms. Reduced-motion mode shortens presentation and reactions. Every step is timed by `pause()` rather than animation frames, and the stage samples the same time-based tracks whenever it renders, so hidden tabs still complete. Restart/mission selection cancels an in-flight presentation; visual effects and phase banners never intercept input. See the repository [asset rights and provenance](../../../ASSETS.md) and [license](../../../LICENSE). Local-only originals and candidates remain untouched.

Sound is on by default (♫); because of browser autoplay policy, music starts at the first pointer or key gesture. Turning ♫ off is remembered in `localStorage` (`ember-grid:sound`) across reloads. Chapter music loops at a 7% base volume, scaled by the BGM setting: `monster-assault` for chapters 1–3, `monster-assault-2` for 4–5 and `final-boss` for 6. Each role's existing attack WAV accompanies its cue, scaled by the SFX setting; `healer_attack.wav` also plays for staff healing and as quiet potion feedback, so it is always prepared. Voices are bounded to four. Music stops at victory/defeat and resumes only during an active battle. Hidden tabs keep playing: battle presentation completes on a time basis while hidden (no `requestAnimationFrame`/Web Animations dependency, waits resolve immediately), so enemy turns keep advancing, and music/voices continue so a backgrounded battle stays audible. Muting stops all voices. No generation, transcoding or new sound publication occurs. See [music provenance](../../../public/tower-defense-music/README.md) and [WAV provenance](../../../public/tower-defense-sfx/README.md). Subjective listening quality is separate from browser playback/decoding checks.

Chapter models start loading only after the player chooses a destination, including a suspended game's chapter. Battle input remains unavailable until models are ready and the WebGL context is available. Restart or chapter selection during preparation starts preparation for the selected battle again; failed model loads offer retry. Ending replay also offers retry, and restart and chapter selection remain disabled throughout the replay. If WebGL cannot be created, the title and settings remain available; the player can check browser hardware acceleration and retry without changing the save. Missing sound effects do not block the battle: a notice appears and loading retries in the background every five seconds; cues not yet loaded are silent.

## Verification

Enemy range previews and the danger overlay use the same possible action positions: holding enemies project only from their current cell. Guard enemies are shown conservatively with their full movement reach even before alert, because they may become alerted during the enemy phase; an unalerted guard that does not alert remains stationary. Attack and healing presentation use the action's explicit target, so level-up HP growth is not presented as a hit or heal.

npm run typecheck

npm run lint
npx prettier --check examples/games/ember-grid
