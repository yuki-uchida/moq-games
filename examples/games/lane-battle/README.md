# うんこ大惨事 / Lane Battle

Independent local single-player lane strategy campaign with a poop theme: 30 stages, about two hours. The poop army defends the うんこ塔 on the right and marches left on the enemy fort, a castle-sized toilet. The enemies are everything that wants poop flushed, cleaned or settled: 清掃員, モップのおばちゃん, 和式便器, 洋式便器 (whose water shot knocks units back), ヨーグルト, 白湯, 整腸剤 and the heavy 下痢止め. The enemy fort fires a ウォシュレット砲 at nearby poops, sends two 朝の清掃 waves of 清掃員 at 10 and 30 seconds (bigger in later restrooms, no 💩 bounty) and a 大掃除 rush each time its HP passes 4/5, 3/5, 2/5 and 1/5. Both forts and the whole battlefield stay visible without scrolling.

The fun is formation (編成), growth (育成) and the world:

- **Formation.** Up to six roster units go into battle; their order is the summon-button order (keys 1–6). Two poops are owned at the start; four more are bought with 🧻 earned from victories once enough stages are cleared. Each first clear has a 30% chance to add one random **special unit** — something bad for the stomach: 冷房, ラーメン, 夜ふかし, お酒, 二郎系ラーメン, 焼肉, ニンニク, 激辛カレー, 牛乳, 生ガキ, アイスの食べすぎ, エナジードリンク — with its own trick (slow, splash, knockback, critical hits, rapid fire). Unowned specials come first, then duplicates join as fresh copies.
- **Growth.** Every unit that damaged a fallen enemy earns its EXP; a victory adds a clear bonus to the whole formation, a defeat keeps only kill EXP. Each level rolls HP, 攻 and 速 (attack speed) against growth chances. Poops grow reliably (80/75/40%, never an empty level). Specials join stronger — +3% per stage cleared, so about 1.9× by the last stage — but grow rarely (22/20/10%), so older specials fall behind and get replaced while poops stay useful.
- **World.** Five restrooms of six stages each: 公園のトイレ, 駅ナカトイレ, 学校のトイレ, 病院のトイレ and 黄金の便器城. Each introduces new enemies; fort HP, enemy strength and spawn rate climb steadily.

In battle: summon cards cost 💩 and recharge, 腸活 (U) raises 💩 income up to Lv.5 for 100/250/450/700 💩, but each level makes enemies spawn 7% faster and adds six cleaners to every 朝の清掃 wave still to come — investing at the start loses or slows the win, so hold the morning first and invest once a front line stands; 大放屁 hurts and pushes back enemies in the home half every 24 seconds, Space pauses. Progress (cleared stages, 🧻, roster, formation and best times) is saved locally under `unko-daisanji-campaign-v1`; storage failure does not prevent play. 作戦室 between battles shows the result report (EXP, level-ups with their stat gains, the new special), formation and shop; its button leads to the full-screen ステージ選択 map. The map winds through the five restrooms and shows only reached stages, each with its boss (the newest enemy, the strongest with a crown on each restroom's last stage) standing on a numbered pedestal, plus the next stage as a silhouette. Tap a stage to select it, then ブリッと出撃; 編成・購入 returns to the 作戦室.

Play length is checked with `bot.js`, a reference player with two modes. The **human-paced** mode taps once per second on a random ready card, invests in 腸活 only to Lv.2 and only after both 朝の清掃 waves, and uses 大放屁 late; each battle adds 45 seconds in the 作戦室 (`MENU_SECONDS`). Playing the campaign in order and retrying on defeat (grinding EXP), six seeds take 93.6–123.2 minutes (mean 107.3). Over 24 other seeds, the human-paced mean is 118.4 minutes with 7.3 defeats, and the **greedy** mode (every 50ms, everything affordable, 腸活 to Lv.3 after the morning) averages 122.2 minutes with 8.1 defeats. The greedy mean exceeds the 121-minute target. Summon recharge is the listed `cooldown` × `COOLDOWN_SCALE` (1.8), so cards cannot be spammed. Wins last about one and a half to two and a half minutes: the march (`WALK_PACE`), fort HP and four 大掃除 rushes set the length of each siege. The tables live in `simulation.js` (units, enemies, stage curves) and `campaign.js` (EXP, growth, rewards).

Attacks use unit-specific single targets, impact circles, forward bands, cones, piercing lines, shockwaves, rings and bounces. Ranged and delayed strikes retain their original recipients after release; a dead recipient is skipped. Damage credits every contributing card. Fort attacks deal primary damage only, although impact zones can still be created there.

Battles advance in fixed 1/60-second ticks independent of rendering frame rate. Attacks, damage-over-time ticks, zones and projectiles resolve in timestamp order within each tick. The first fort destroyed decides the battle; if both fall at exactly the same time, the player wins.

Status effects include movement slow, control, attack-speed changes, damage modifiers, burns, poison, timed explosions, displacement, execution and healing. Control effects do not overlap and grant a short immunity afterwards; heavy units resist control and displacement, while ドデカ cannot be displaced or launched. Only the strongest movement slow applies. Zones are replaced per source, persist after its death, and expire; damage-over-time ticks do not cause hit flinches. Unit-specific details and values are in `UNITS.md`.

The battlefield shows every attack's real area as it lands, in the attacker's own style (`visual-effects.js`): 冷房's cold gust fills its cone, 洋式便器's water beam reaches its farthest victim, 宿便大王's golden crest runs along the floor and hits each foe as it passes, 二郎系's floor ring launches everyone around it, splashes and bursts match the impact radius, and コロコロ's pellet bounces and rolls to the next foe. Special effects are visible too: drain drops and heal sparkles with a green ＋, collision sparks, the 激辛 blast, the 号令 ring, a whirlpool under a flushed poop, landing dust. Status badges (at most three, by priority) sit above HP bars, with drawn on-body cues (ice block, orbiting pills, spiral, Z's, crown, flames, poison bubbles, a chili that pulses faster before it blows); mud, stench and wet-floor zones lie on the floor under the units. Displacements slide to their new position and launched units rise above the lane. Everything runs on the simulation clock, freezes while paused and thins out under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Roster and shop cards show attack areas and effect names; summon-card tooltips include their descriptions.

Browser checks use installed Chrome headlessly and need the development server. Controls: tap/click cards, 1–6 to summon, U to upgrade, Space to pause (keyboard only; there is no on-screen pause button). The ♪ button toggles all sound. An original quiet 16-step Web Audio melody and bass accompany combat. Oscillator envelopes distinguish summon, production upgrade, pellet release, impact, burst and results; no sound files or network service are required. Sound begins after a user gesture and retries unlocking on later gestures if the browser suspends or interrupts audio. Mute cancels current voices as well as future notes; pause, results and hidden tabs stop the music. Resume continues without a catch-up burst. When audio is unavailable, play continues silently. Buttons also give quiet synthesized hover/tap/select/confirm cues (`examples/games/shared/UiSound.ts`), a press scale, and a short pop on a successful summon, upgrade or burst; they follow the same mute.

## Visuals

Everything is drawn in code; the game loads no image or model files. `models.js` builds all 26 characters from three.js primitives in a toon style (three-band shading, inverted-hull outlines, glossy highlights, groove shading): six poops on a coiled swirl body (kabuto and shield, headbands, bandana, haramaki, a crowned king with ermine cape and golden plunger), twelve specials (an air conditioner, ramen bowls, a sleepy smartphone, a sake bottle, a grill, garlic, curry, a milk carton, an oyster, ice cream, an energy drink) and eight enemies (cleaners with broom and mop, side-on squat and western toilets, a yogurt cup, a teacup, a capsule, a medicine bottle). Each character is one skeleton — a bending spine chain plus rigid bones for arms, props, lids and faces — and every part is skinned and merged into one lit mesh (face ink and unlit highlights ride along as per-vertex shading modes), one translucent mesh and one outline, so a character costs at most three draw calls. Actors are pooled per character and reused, and a disposed one frees its bone texture, so a long battle allocates no new GPU resources once its cast has appeared. The rig lives in the lane frame, so lunges travel along the lane and swings stay in the screen plane while bodies are turned toward the viewer. Menu portraits use the same models; a single offscreen WebGL renderer draws them with an orthographic camera in the lane's 1000×450 space, and the 2D canvas composites that image above the restroom backdrop, toilet-paper road and shadows, and below HP bars, pellets and splashes.

Motion runs on the simulation clock (`animation.js`) without changing it. Damage lands (or a shot is released) at attack phase 0, so each character's acting holds its contact pose exactly then: a slower anticipation in the last part of the previous period, a strike of a few frames, a short contact hold, a rebound and recovery. Each recipe defines its own anticipation and hold from `UNITS.md`; anticipation is capped at 55% of the scheduled cycle. A walking unit starts winding up as it closes in, so even its first strike is anticipated, and an attack interrupted by the target falling eases out. Each character acts with its own body and props — shield bash, overhand throw, headbutt dash, splash, belly bump, royal plunger slam, cold blast, chopstick stab, phone slap, sake pour, ground pound, flaring grill, stink puff, chili jab, carton tackle, shell snap, scoop headbutt, can recoil, overhead broom smash, mop sweep, lid slap, cup splash, basin lurch, lid nod with water jet, capsule pop and bottle slam — and the variants it needs: a ready ゲリラ奇襲 lunges further and glows red, 夜ふかし alternates forehand and backhand, crits follow through further, エナジー shakes harder with its caffeine ramp, 激辛カレー heats up while winding. Shots leave the shooter's emitter; ordinary ranged attacks travel 120ms while cone, piercing, shockwave and bounce hits use their own arrival times.

Every character also reacts and dies in its own way (recipe hooks `hurt`, `status` and `ko` in `models.js`, with 2D extras in `HURT_VFX` and `DEATH_VFX` of `visual-effects.js`). A hit starts on the hit frame and settles within 0.55 s (0.7 s for heavy units) on top of any attack in progress; criticals, knockback and heavy blows hold the jolt longer, pop the body up and keep the ＞＜ face longer, weak hits barely flash, and the flash never covers the face ink, so white characters still show their ＞＜. Each character reacts in its own way: カチカチ guards with its shield, コロコロ curls up, ドデカ's belly ripples, 宿便大王's crown pops, the smartphone's screen cracks, the oyster clams up, the cleaner staggers with the cap askew, the toilet lid slams. Deaths last 0.8–1.15 s (`koSeconds`) and fade over their last quarter: helmets, crowns, caps, lids and cloves fly off on their own bones, puddles spread under bodies that melt, spill or are crushed, the oyster and the squat toilet sink through the floor (cut at the floor line), grills and air conditioners smoke, the sleepless phone dozes off, the cleaner sees stars. Control states have poses: frozen units hold their pose in pale blue, stunned ones sway, launched ones tumble, confused ones wobble backwards, drowsy ones droop their eyelids.

The battle line is laid out for crowds: each side stands on its own three ground rows, interleaved so opposing units never share a depth; within a row the unit nearest the enemy is drawn last, and every unit has its own depth slot, so neighbours never cut into each other. A unit is drawn behind its logical point by a stand-off (its own, or its body's breadth for wide characters; back rows a step further), so facing fighters keep faces and bodies apart; the stand-off grows on large-scale screens so a melee looks the same everywhere. Each melee strike then lunges just far enough for its striking point (weapon tip or striking body, measured on the model's contact pose) to meet the front of the foe's drawn body at that height, so blows connect without bodies overlapping; hit splats and shots land on that front, splashing in the shot's own colour (orange on a critical). Health bars appear once a unit is hurt and sit beneath the characters. The simulation, ranges and timing are unchanged. Portrait screens draw the armies larger.

The root landing page links to this example, and `npm run build` includes its entry. The dedicated Vite configuration outputs to `dist-lane-battle`; root typecheck targets the tower game, while this example uses JavaScript and simulation/browser checks. Dense armies can overlap. All stages use the same full-width lane, tinted per restroom; there is no multiplayer or mid-battle save.

## Background progression

An active battle continues through a shared monotonic clock. Besides animation frames, a 250ms heartbeat from a Web Worker timer (which escapes main-thread background throttling) keeps it running while the tab is hidden or the window is occluded; a plain `setInterval` is the fallback when workers are unavailable. On return, missed time is simulated in steps of at most 50ms, preserving combat rules, the 999 resource limit and terminal results. Each callback processes at most 30 seconds of simulation and schedules another batch until it catches up or the battle ends; battle inputs are blocked while reconciliation is pending. Explicit pause and results never accrue simulation debt, and starting/replaying a stage resets the clock. Hidden and reconciled combat produces no catch-up sounds.

Browsers and operating systems can throttle, freeze or discard background pages. Immediate background execution is therefore best effort; discarded pages cannot restore an unsaved battle. Long foreground stalls are reconciled in the same bounded batches. Separate tabs keep independent games. The automated background check covers skipped callbacks, explicit pause and eventual result after a long suspension, plus a real Chrome page freeze/resume and a headless tab switch; headless Chrome may keep both tabs visible, so that switch alone does not prove OS background suspension.
